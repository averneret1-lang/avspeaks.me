North Star AI Vision

What a good one contains

A North Star AI Vision is a short document that tells a company where AI fits and in what order to do the work. I have written one for more than 10 companies. Each one has these nine parts.

Part oneDirection

1 · The North Star

One sentence that says where the business will be, and by when.

  • It names a business result. It does not name a tool.
  • It has a date and a number.
  • Business and technical teams can both repeat it.

2 · The baseline

Today’s numbers, recorded before anyone builds anything.

  • Three to five measures that leaders already care about.
  • Each number comes from the company’s own systems.
  • Each measure has a target and a date.

3 · The priorities

A short list of AI work, ranked by what it does for the business.

  • Revenue, operations, and risk are in the same list.
  • The first project is a task that people do often and dislike.
  • The list also says what the company will not do yet.

Part twoFoundation

4 · Clean data

AI can only be as correct as the records it reads.

  • Each important fact has one home and one owner.
  • Old and duplicate records are fixed before a task is automated.
  • The document names the systems an agent may read.

5 · Governance rules

Written rules that keep people in control of the decisions that matter.

  • AI proposes. A person approves anything that reaches a customer or changes a number.
  • An agent shows its sources and marks what it could not confirm.
  • An agent stops when the numbers do not reconcile.
  • Sensitive data stays in approved systems.

6 · Ownership

A named person for every result, data set, and agent.

  • A business leader owns the North Star and its measures.
  • Security approves data access before a pilot starts.
  • Every agent has one owner who maintains it.

Part threePeople and delivery

7 · Adoption

A reason for each person to use it, starting in the first week.

  • The first agent gives time back to the people who run it.
  • It works for someone with no AI experience.
  • Leaders use it in front of their teams.
  • Peers show each other what worked, and good work is recognized.

8 · Skills

A regular time for leaders and teams to practice on their own work.

  • Sessions happen every week.
  • People bring a real task from their job.
  • Leaders build their skills first, then their teams.

9 · Roadmap and review

A plan in small steps, checked against the baseline.

  • The first 90 days prove one workflow with a pilot team.
  • Only work that moved its measure is extended.
  • Results are measured, and logins are not counted as results.
  • Agents that people do not use are retired.

Each vision I write connects AI to revenue, operations, and risk, and gives business and technical teams one shared direction.
